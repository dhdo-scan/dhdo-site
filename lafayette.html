Why Lafayette

Why Lafayette Homes Need Documentation

Lafayette Parish’s worst disaster in decades wasn’t a hurricane — and that’s exactly what makes the real risk easy to underestimate. Between recurring flooding and a steady run of Gulf storms, the question here isn’t really “what if” — it’s “when,” and “will I be ready.”

01

The Parish’s Defining Disaster Wasn’t a Hurricane

In August 2016, an unnamed rain system stalled over Acadiana for two days, dropping more than 20 inches of rain and pushing the Vermilion River to its highest level since 1940. An estimated 4,000 to 5,000 homes across Lafayette Parish flooded. Add two decades of Gulf storms on top of that — Rita in 2005, Gustav and Ike in 2008, Laura and Delta in 2020 — and the region’s real exposure runs well beyond hurricane season.

02

Records Get Lost in the Chaos

After a flood or a storm, floors get ripped out, damaged items get hauled to the curb before anyone thinks to photograph them, and receipts get lost in the scramble. A homeowner reconstructing what they owned from memory, weeks or months later, is starting from a disadvantage that was avoidable.

03

Built Before You Need It

A DHDO scan is meant to help avoid that disadvantage. It’s built before the water rises or the wind picks up, stored off-site, and ready the moment a roof leaks, a fence goes down, or a home floods — regardless of what happens to the physical house itself.

Serving Lafayette Parish

DHDO scans homes, rental properties, and local businesses throughout Lafayette and the surrounding Lafayette Parish communities, including these areas:

Downtown & River Ranch

Established Lafayette neighborhoods — River Ranch sits directly along the Vermilion River corridor — home to properties with decades of accumulated furnishings, art, and valuables worth documenting in detail.

Broussard & Youngsville

Growing communities across Lafayette Parish, including upscale developments like Youngsville’s Sugar Mill Pond, sitting in some of the parish’s fastest-growing flood-risk corridors — homes in Youngsville’s Highland Ridge subdivision took on up to 20 inches of water in the 2016 flood.

Scott & Carencro

Inland Lafayette Parish communities where wind, rain, and drainage — not storm surge — drive the real risk; homes near Scott’s Country Living Estates have flooded more than once in the past 15 years.

We serve the surrounding Greater Lafayette area and the rest of Lafayette Parish. If you’re a homeowner, HOA, or business outside these named areas, reach out — we’re likely already scanning nearby.

Our Process

How Your Lafayette Scan Works

Booking takes one phone call. From there, we handle everything — most Lafayette homes and businesses are fully documented in a single visit.

1
Step One

Schedule Your Scan

Call or fill out the form. Every inquiry gets a response within 12 hours, and most scans are booked within days.

2
Step Two

We Scan Your Property

One visit, start to finish. A DHDO operator walks every room, closet, garage, and outbuilding — or every classroom and common area, for a business — with our 3D camera. Nothing is touched or moved.

Assets Get Tagged
