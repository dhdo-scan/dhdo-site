What We Do

A Pre-Storm Record Built to Hold Up

A strong hurricane claim needs one thing above all: dated, room-by-room proof that predates the storm — not an estimate of what your kitchen held, and not a guess at what your furniture or equipment was worth. A record made before the storm arrived, independent of anyone's account of it afterward, captured by a third party rather than self-reported. That record may help support your position if a claim is ever disputed. DHDO's documentation is built around three things:

A Complete 3D Walkthrough

Every room and outbuilding captured in navigable detail before storm season — a record your adjuster can revisit anytime, from anywhere, instead of a stack of phone photos taken in a hurry.

More Than a Home Inventory Checklist

Make, model, serial number, and purchase date attached to the items that matter — a home inventory list for insurance claims that's already finished before you ever need it, not a paper checklist you have to build and maintain yourself.

Timestamped & Verified

Every scan carries an independent date and is captured by our team — so there's no question about when the record was made or who made it, and no risk of guessing after the fact.

Not Just Homes

Local Businesses Carry the Same Risk

Churches, schools, apartments, and other local businesses across Southwest Louisiana face the same exposure a home does — often with more square footage, equipment, and inventory riding on it. The same room-by-room process applies; we simply scope the visit to the size of the property. If you're documenting a multi-unit building or community facility, mention it when you book so we can plan the visit accordingly.

The Problem

Why South Louisiana Hurricane Claims Fall Short

The Gulf Coast has weathered Katrina, Rita, Laura, and Delta — and in Southwest Louisiana, the pattern after each storm looks the same, whether it's a family living room or a church fellowship hall: someone trying to describe what a space looked like, what was inside it, and what it was worth, working only from recollection. A claim built on nothing but recollection rarely holds up the same way one built on a dated, independent record does.

01

When an Adjuster Has to Take Your Word for It

Without a pre-loss record, an adjuster is often working from a homeowner's or business owner's account of what a space held and what it was worth, filed weeks or months after the storm. A dated record gives them something to check against, instead of something to take on faith.

02

When Wind and Flood Become a Coverage Argument

A single hurricane routinely causes both wind and flood damage, and the two are typically covered by separate policies. Without proof of pre-storm condition, disputes over which damage falls under which policy can stretch on for months. A record made before landfall gives both sides a fixed point of reference.

03

When Coverage Hasn't Kept Up With Replacement Cost

Rebuilding costs shift every year, but policy limits don't always follow. Without a documented, itemized account of what's actually in a home or building, it's harder to show that a payout is falling short of what replacement actually costs today.
